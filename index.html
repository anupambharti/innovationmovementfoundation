About Us
Who We Are
A 100% digital platform for innovation, sustainability & skill-building.
Established February 2026 · Section 8 Not-for-Profit

Innovation Movement Foundation works towards developing creative thinking, problem-solving skills, financial literacy, research awareness, and innovation among students, youth, and researchers.

The Foundation connects education with real-life skills through digital learning, practical activities, innovation challenges, and sustainable Innovation Labs built from discarded and waste materials. It encourages young minds to observe problems, think creatively, understand money wisely, and convert simple ideas into useful solutions for the Nation and the world.

Through certificates, appreciation awards, workshops, and digital platforms, we nurture confident students who become solution-makers, innovators, and responsible citizens of the future.

🎯 Our Mission
To empower students, youth, and researchers with creative thinking, problem-solving, financial literacy, and innovation skills — helping them turn simple ideas and waste into practical, real, sustainable solutions for the Nation.
🔭 Our Vision
To develop a strong culture of innovation, creative thinking, and problem-solving — from school to employment and entrepreneurship.

Program 01

Problem-Solving Skills

Build creative thinking and real-world problem-solving.

Choose any one · Write 100–150 words & send
  • Future Foods. What will be on our plate?
  • The Circular Economy. How can business introduce more circular processes?
  • Air Pollution. Who is responsible?
  • Gen Z. How powerful are they?
  • Leadership for Change. The role of leadership, entrepreneurial spirit, and change-makers.
Program 02

Financial Literacy

Practical money-management skills for students and young professionals.

Choose any one · Write 100–150 words & submit
  • Part-Time Earning. If you could earn money while studying, what work would you choose and why?
  • Pocket Money. If you receive ₹1,000, how will you divide it among spending, saving, investing, and giving?
  • Saving & Investing. How can saving and investing together build a financially secure future? (Or: why is money in a piggy bank different from money in a bank, bonds, or stocks?)
  • Needs & Wants. How does knowing the difference help you manage money wisely?
  • Money & the Nation. How can wise saving and investment help businesses, create jobs, and support the country's growth?

Program 03 · Innovation Lab
My Innovation Lab
Innovation Lab Worksheet • Ages 10–17. Spot a problem, design a solution, test it with others.
Part A

General Innovation Process

Every great invention starts with someone noticing a problem worth solving. Work through five steps: spotting real problems, choosing one, generating ideas, narrowing to something testable, and getting feedback. Use words or sketches — whatever helps you think.

Step 1Spot the Problems
Think about daily life — at home, school, online, or in your neighbourhood. Identify 3 problems that are frustrating, inefficient, or harder than they should be.
Step 2Choose Your Problem
Review your 3 problems and choose the ONE you most want to work on.
Check your assumption. Write 3–4 questions, then ask at least 3 people and record their answers.
Survey Results Log
Step 3Generate Ideas
Brainstorm freely — quantity over quality. Imagine unlimited time, tools, and support. Capture at least 2 directions.
Step 4Narrow It Down
Choose your strongest idea, then scale it down to the smallest version you could test this week using things you already have.
Step 5Get Feedback
Present your idea to your team in 1–2 minutes. Record their feedback and any new suggestions.
Teammate Feedback
Part B

Waste to Wonder

Every day, useful materials get thrown away — plastic, cardboard, cloth, metal, glass, electronics, food scraps. Look closely at waste, understand the problem it creates, and design a brand-new product made from it.

Step 1Spot the Waste Problem
Identify 3 discarded items or types of waste you see often. For each, note where it comes from, where it usually ends up, and why that's a problem.
